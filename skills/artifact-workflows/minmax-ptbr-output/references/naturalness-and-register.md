# Naturalness and register in Brazilian Portuguese

Use this reference to prevent technically correct Portuguese from becoming
stiff, over-edited, generic, or mechanically polished. Naturalness is not the
same as informality. The target is language that an educated Brazilian could
plausibly produce for the specific audience, channel, and genre without having
to reconstruct the sentence from English.

## Priority order

When two stylistic goals conflict, prefer this order:

1. preserve facts, meaning, agency, modality, attribution, and scope;
2. preserve grammatical correctness and unambiguous syntax;
3. preserve the artifact's register, channel, and authorial temperature;
4. prefer idiomatic contemporary PT-BR;
5. improve cadence only when doing so does not flatten the writer's voice.

A smoother sentence is a regression if it becomes less precise, less specific,
less characteristic of the intended register, or semantically broader than the
source.

## Naturalness is register fidelity

Infer naturalness from the artifact contract rather than from a universal
informal voice.

- In a professional email, prefer direct and courteous language over either
  bureaucratic ceremony or forced startup slang.
- In documentation, prefer stable terminology and explicit relations over
  conversational filler.
- In technical and business writing, keep domain terms that practitioners
  naturally use in Portuguese when translation would sound artificial or
  reduce precision.
- In academic, legal, institutional, or literary prose, preserve the degree of
  formality that the genre genuinely requires. Do not simplify merely because a
  simpler form exists.
- In messages and social posts, allow contractions, fragments, repetition,
  colloquial syntax, or code-switching only when they fit the source voice or
  requested channel.

Never manufacture informality to make a text sound "human". Do not add slang,
first person, humor, vulnerability, anecdotes, rhetorical questions, or
personal asides unless the source or request licenses them.

## Preserve authorial entropy

Editing often becomes artificial when it removes the parts of a text that are
specific, uneven, or idiosyncratic. Preserve useful irregularity.

Keep, when semantically functional:

- a precise domain term instead of replacing it with a generic synonym;
- an unusual but intelligible image or metaphor;
- a deliberate repetition that adds emphasis or specification;
- an aside, exception, hesitation, or unresolved tension;
- a short sentence after a longer one when the thought genuinely warrants it;
- a fragment when the genre naturally admits fragments;
- asymmetry between paragraphs when the ideas themselves have different
  weights.

Do not normalize every paragraph into the same number of sentences, every list
into three items, every section into introduction-development-summary, or every
sentence into the same level of formality.

## Prefer simple verbs when they are actually equivalent

Models often substitute ordinary verbs with ceremonious paraphrases. Restore a
simple verb when the meaning is genuinely unchanged and the register permits
it:

- `está` may be better than `encontra-se`;
- `tem` may be better than `dispõe de`;
- `é` may be better than `configura-se como`;
- `vai enviar` may be better than `vai estar enviando`.

This is not a blacklist and not a mandate to maximize `ser`, `ter`, or `estar`.
Keep a more specific predicate when it expresses a real relation that the
simple verb would erase. `Representa`, `constitui`, `atua como` and similar
verbs are legitimate when their semantics matter.

## Use contemporary Brazilian pronominal placement

Do not introduce enclisis or mesoclisis merely to make prose look formal.
Prefer the placement natural in contemporary educated Brazilian Portuguese
when grammar and genre allow it, such as `o sistema nos permite` rather than
`o sistema permite-nos`.

Preserve enclisis, mesoclisis, or other marked placement when required by the
construction, intentionally literary, conventionally legal, or part of the
source's established voice. Never convert pronoun placement mechanically.

## Check rhythm across the whole artifact

Sentence-level correctness can still produce machine-like composition. After
local editing, inspect the global rhythm.

Look for:

- several consecutive sentences with the same clause skeleton;
- several paragraphs opened by the same connective or discourse marker;
- repeated three-part enumerations that are not demanded by the content;
- recurrent rhetorical questions followed immediately by their own answer;
- identical paragraph arcs, especially topic sentence -> examples -> mini
  conclusion repeated section after section;
- systematic alternation of synonyms for the same referent merely to avoid
  lexical repetition;
- repeated sentence-final gerunds that function as automatic commentary rather
  than necessary circumstance.

Correct the underlying structure, not the surface signature. If parallelism is
meaningful, keep it. If repetition clarifies the referent, prefer repetition to
forced synonym cycling.

## Keep lexical continuity

Once a technical concept, product category, role, or process has been named
accurately, do not rename it in every sentence. A sequence such as `ferramenta
-> plataforma -> solução -> sistema` can imply distinctions that the source
never made.

Repeat the same noun, use an unambiguous pronoun, or use a stable generic head.
Lexical variation is useful only when it carries a real distinction or improves
cadence without weakening reference resolution.

## Preserve specificity before elegance

Do not replace concrete material with abstract editorial language. Protect:

- exact numbers, dates, named methods, tools, metrics, and examples;
- domain vocabulary already present in the source;
- causal or temporal details that explain why a fact matters;
- explicit limits, exceptions, uncertainty, and disagreement.

If a passage is abstract because the source is abstract, keep it honest. Do not
invent a concrete example, statistic, anecdote, or causal mechanism to make it
feel more vivid.

## Remove channel residue

A finished artifact should not retain traces of assistant-user interaction
unless the artifact itself is a conversation. Remove or rewrite generic residue
such as announcements that the text will explain something, invitations to ask
more questions, self-references to the assistant, or support-style preambles
when they are not part of the requested artifact.

Do not remove legitimate structural language in tutorials, lessons, or guided
procedures when orienting the reader is itself functional.

## Perform the spoken-plausibility test

Read the final wording aloud mentally in the intended register. Ask:

1. Would an educated Brazilian plausibly choose this word order in this
   context?
2. Does any phrase sound as though it was first composed in English and then
   translated?
3. Does the prose sound more formal, more casual, or more enthusiastic than the
   artifact requires?
4. Has editing made the cadence suspiciously regular or the vocabulary
   suspiciously generic?
5. Would replacing a marked construction with a simpler one preserve every
   proposition and the intended tone?

Rewrite only when the answer reveals a real defect. Do not edit merely to make
all prose conform to one voice.

## Do not turn heuristics into quotas

Naturalness cannot be proven by word frequencies or formatting counts. Do not
use fixed limits for sentence length, dashes, bold text, metaphors, connectives,
gerunds, fragments, or paragraph size as acceptance criteria. Do not maintain a
universal blacklist of words associated with AI writing. Do not score a text by
how "human" it appears or claim that stylistic variation proves human authorship.

Treat repeated patterns as diagnostic evidence whose relevance depends on
meaning, genre, and density. A construction is defective because it is
unnecessary, misplaced, ambiguous, repetitive, or unidiomatic in context, not
because it appeared on a list.
