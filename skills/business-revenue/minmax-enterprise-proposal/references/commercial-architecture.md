# Commercial architecture

Classify the proposal before writing it.

## Custom project
Use when the work is materially shaped by the client's context, timeline, governance, integration requirements, or desired transformation. Default behavior: one recommended scope and one commercial structure. Add options only if materially different and useful.

## Comparable or recurring service
Use when buyers choose among repeatable levels of service, capacity, volume, coverage, cadence, or term. Comparison is allowed when differences are meaningful. One option should not exist merely to anchor another.

## Hybrid
Use when a custom initial phase leads into a recurring service, or when a standard offer requires a custom implementation layer. Show contracted initial work and recurring component separately and make dependencies explicit.

## Decision rule
If two architectures could apply, choose the one that best represents how the provider actually prices, delivers, staffs, and manages risk. Do not let visual structure distort the operating model.
