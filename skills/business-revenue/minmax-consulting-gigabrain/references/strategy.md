# Strategy framework library

Use the minimum framework set needed. Sources are in `framework-source-ledger.md`.

## 1. Strategy Palette (F-009)

Use first when the environment itself determines how strategy should be developed.

Assess:
- predictability of demand, technology, regulation and competitor behavior;
- malleability: can the firm materially shape the environment/ecosystem?;
- harshness: are resources or viability severely constrained?

Route:
- predictable + low malleability -> classical positioning/advantage;
- unpredictable + low malleability -> adaptive experimentation;
- predictable + high malleability -> visionary creation;
- unpredictable + high malleability -> shaping/ecosystem orchestration;
- severe resource constraint -> renewal first.

Anti-pattern: using a five-year deterministic plan in a highly unpredictable adaptive environment.

## 2. Five Forces (F-006)

Use to understand industry structure and economic-value pressure, not to generate a generic five-row table.

For each force:
- identify the economic mechanism;
- quantify evidence where possible;
- distinguish current state from trend;
- state profit-pool implication;
- identify what is structurally changeable versus merely observable.

Output: force -> evidence -> trend -> economic implication -> strategic response.

Do not confuse a company's strength with industry attractiveness.

## 3. Strategic positioning, trade-offs and fit (F-007)

A strategy must identify:
- target customers/needs;
- distinctive value proposition;
- relative price or relative cost advantage sought;
- activities configured differently;
- explicit trade-offs / what the company will not do;
- reinforcing fit across activities.

Reject strategies that are only aspirations, goals, best-practice adoption or lists of initiatives without choices.

## 4. Value Chain (F-008)

Use when the question is where cost, differentiation, bottlenecks or capability advantage actually arise.

Disaggregate into strategically relevant activities, then map:
- cost drivers;
- willingness-to-pay/value drivers;
- dependencies and handoffs;
- activities performed differently from competitors;
- upstream/downstream value-system effects.

Avoid benchmarking an activity without checking whether the business model requires it to be structurally different.

## 5. Three Horizons (F-004)

Use to balance current performance, emerging businesses and future options concurrently.

Do not treat horizons as a simple calendar. Classify by maturity/value-creation role and required management system.

For each horizon record:
- strategic role;
- capital/talent allocation;
- evidence threshold;
- metrics appropriate to maturity;
- transition criteria to the next horizon;
- kill criteria.

## 6. Portfolio of Initiatives (F-005)

Use when uncertainty makes one monolithic forecast unreliable. Maintain a portfolio of differently timed, differently risky initiatives/options.

For each initiative:
- thesis;
- uncertainty level;
- reversible vs irreversible commitment;
- next information milestone;
- investment tranche;
- upside/downside;
- kill/scale trigger.

## 7. Growth-Share Matrix (F-010)

Use only as a first-pass portfolio cash-allocation diagnostic. Define market, growth and relative share carefully.

Never infer `invest/divest` from quadrant alone. Add economics, strategic fit, advantage, capital intensity, market structure and future scenarios before a recommendation.
